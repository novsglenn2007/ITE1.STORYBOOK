Empty Chairs at Sunday Dinner

by Marguerite Doyle

  • Genre: Drama
  • Chapter: The Fourth Chair
  • Reading time: about 1 min

Synopsis

Three years after their father's passing, the Alvarez siblings gather for a Sunday dinner no one wants to host, and the real conversation begins with the last place setting.

Chapter 1 of 1

The Fourth Chair

Carmen set the table for five, out of habit, and then stood still with the last plate in her hands. There were only four of them now: herself, her brothers Luis and Mateo, and their mother, who had insisted on cooking the old family adobo even though her hands shook as she stirred.

For three years, the Sunday dinners had been brief and careful, like walking over thin ice. Nobody mentioned Papa's chair at the head of the table. Nobody sat in it. Tonight the empty seat felt louder than any argument.

"I'm moving it," Luis said suddenly, standing up. "I can't stare at it anymore." Mateo slammed his palm on the table. "You don't get to decide that." Their mother, quietly, put down her spoon. "Your father hated that chair. It squeaked. He only kept it because you three would fight to sit beside him."

Silence. Then Carmen laughed, a small, startled sound, and the laughter spread until all four of them were wiping their eyes. They did not move the chair. But when Mateo's little daughter ran in, late and loud, she climbed straight up into it, swinging her feet, and nobody said a word. The adobo, finally, was eaten while it was still warm.
