Letters from the Harbor, 1898

by Isabela Reyes-Cruz

  • Genre: Historical Fiction
  • Chapter: The Clerk at Desk Six
  • Reading time: about 1 min

Synopsis

In the turbulent summer of 1898, a young telegraph clerk in Manila secretly copies messages that could save a neighbor's family.

Chapter 1 of 1

The Clerk at Desk Six

Ramon had worked at the harbor telegraph office for exactly four months, and in that time he had learned two rules: be quick, and never read what you send. In the hot, uncertain summer of 1898, with rumors flying faster than the wires, the second rule felt harder every day.

On a stifling afternoon, a message arrived addressed to a name he knew: Señora Dolores Alba, the widow who lived above his family's shop and sold him pan de sal on credit. The message was short and urgent. Her son's ship had been delayed, and an order had been issued to search the district for travelers connected to certain letters.

Ramon looked at the clock. His shift ended in ten minutes. If he delivered the message officially it would take until morning. He folded the copy into his shirt, left the office with a casual wave, and ran through the narrow streets, past the cathedral bells and the water sellers, to the Alba door.

"You must burn the letters in your trunk," he panted, handing her the paper. The widow looked at him for a long moment, then pressed a warm roll into his palm. "Come for breakfast tomorrow," she said, "and bring your courage. It's too big to keep at a telegraph desk."
