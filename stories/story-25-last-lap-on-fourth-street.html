Last Lap on Fourth Street

by Rico Salazar

  • Genre: Sports
  • Chapter: The Final Four Hundred Meters
  • Reading time: about 1 min

Synopsis

Cross-country runner Bea has one chance left to beat her rival, and a sprained ankle that says otherwise.

Chapter 1 of 1

The Final Four Hundred Meters

Bea had trained for this race for three years. She had run in the rain, in the dark, in her cousin's too-big sneakers when hers wore out. Now she stood on the starting line of the regional finals, her ankle wrapped tight under her sock, and the girl beside her, Kiara, smiled with the calm of someone who had never lost.

The gun cracked. The first lap felt fine. The second felt hard. By the third, Bea's ankle burned like a hot coal, and Kiara was three strides ahead, her ponytail swinging like a pendulum. Bea heard the crowd as a distant ocean. She thought of quitting. She thought of her father in the bleachers, hands clenched on his cap.

With four hundred meters left, she stopped counting steps and started counting breaths. In, out. In, out. One by one, the strides shortened the gap. On the final curve, she heard Kiara's breathing grow ragged, and something in her chest lifted.

Bea crossed the line a heartbeat late, second place, collapsing onto the grass. Kiara reached down, laughing, and pulled her up. "Next year," she said. Bea, gasping and grinning, said, "Next year."

— The End —
