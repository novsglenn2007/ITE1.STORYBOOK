The Quiet Ration

by Silas Crowe

  • Genre: Dystopian
  • Chapter: Ninety-Nine Words
  • Reading time: about 1 min

Synopsis

In a city where every spoken word is counted and taxed, a clerk named Odel discovers that his daily ration of one hundred words is no longer enough.

Chapter 1 of 1

Ninety-Nine Words

In Concord City, words were rationed at one hundred per citizen per day, issued each dawn by the Ministry of Calm. Odel Marr, a records clerk, had never found this difficult. He lived alone, ate alone, and used his allotment mostly on "yes," "no," and "thank you."

Then one morning, a girl on the tram dropped her ration tag. It rolled to his feet, still glowing, still full. She was perhaps eight years old, and she looked up with enormous eyes and mouthed, without any sound, "Please." Odel understood at once. Children received only twenty words. She had spent hers already.

He knew the law. Giving away words was a crime. But the tram was crowded, the guards were distracted, and Odel's hand moved before his better judgment did. He tapped his tag against hers, transferring sixty of his own words, and watched her face light up like a window at night.

"Thank you," she whispered, the first sentence she had spoken in days. Odel walked home with forty words left and a strange, warm feeling in his chest. He had no idea how he would last until dawn. But he was sure, for the first time in years, that he had said something worth the cost.
