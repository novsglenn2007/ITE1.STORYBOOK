The Wind That Remembered Names

by Alaric Dumalag

  • Genre: Mythology
  • Chapter: The Spirit of the Northern Breeze
  • Reading time: about 1 min

Synopsis

In an island myth retold anew, a wind spirit keeps the names of every sailor lost at sea, until a stubborn girl asks her to give one back.

Chapter 1 of 1

The Spirit of the Northern Breeze

Long before the islands had maps, there was a wind spirit named Hanga, who lived between the sky and the sea. She carried rain to the farms and songs to the fishermen, but her greatest duty was to remember. Whenever a boat did not return, Hanga caught the sailor's name in her hair and held it safe, so that no one would be forgotten.

Over many centuries, her hair grew heavy with names. She floated slowly, humming them to herself, ten thousand of them, a lullaby of the lost.

One evening, a girl named Mayumi climbed the highest cliff and shouted into the breeze. "My father's name was Tomas! Please, can I hear it once?" The spirit hesitated. Names were never returned. They were not meant to be.

But the girl kept calling, night after night, until the wind softened. Hanga bent low and whispered, "Tomas. Fisherman. He laughed loudly and sang off-key." Mayumi wept and smiled at once. Then she did something no one had done before: she said, "Teach me to remember, too."

That is why, even today, when the northern breeze touches your face at dusk, it is said to carry a name. Say it out loud, and the wind will rest.

— The End —
