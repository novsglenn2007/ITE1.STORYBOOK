The Tale of the Singing Rice Terraces

by Elena Dizon

  • Genre: Folklore
  • Chapter: The Song Beneath the Water
  • Reading time: about 1 min

Synopsis

Mountain villagers say the rice terraces sing at harvest, but only to those who work them with honest hands. One greedy lord wants to hear the song for free.

Chapter 1 of 1

The Song Beneath the Water

High in the clouds, where the mountains folded like green waves, there lived a village that tended a thousand terraces of rice. Their elders said that the fields could sing, a low, sweet humming that rose from the flooded paddies at harvest time, but only for those who had earned it with sweat and patience.

One year, a wealthy lord arrived with his servants and his silk shoes. "I will buy the song," he declared. "Name your price." The elders smiled and shook their heads. "It cannot be bought, only grown." The lord scoffed, ordered the villagers to leave, and had his men harvest the terraces by force.

But when the first sickle touched the stalks, the fields fell silent. Not a note. Not a whisper. The grains, when threshed, turned out thin and sour, and the lord's servants went hungry. In rage, he climbed the highest terrace, kicked off his shoes, and sank to his ankles in the cold mud.

For a long time, he stood there, ashamed. Then he began to plant, slowly, clumsily, alongside the villagers who forgave him. The next spring, as the first green shoots rose, he heard it at last: a gentle humming, rising from the water, welcoming him home.

— The End —
